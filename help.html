Help

One section per tool — what it does, how to get started, and the questions people actually get stuck on. Every tool is a single downloadable file (or, for Paperwork Tracker, a workbook plus a companion browser page) — no install, no account, no server.

Micro-Budget Film Budget Builder

Real, sourced 2026 SAG-AFTRA and IATSE rates across all 7 low-budget tiers, plus a Resources tab pointing you to real places to price locations, gear, props, insurance, and legal help.

Getting started: Open the workbook in Excel, Sheets, or Numbers → enter your project basics and pick a budget tier → watch the TIER ALERT banner, which fires the moment something doesn't fit the tier you picked → use the Resources tab when you don't know what a line item should cost → the Top Sheet totals everything automatically.

Non-union? Every tier has a Non-Union path. First time budgeting a film? That's who this is built for — it explains what each tier requires and warns you before a real mistake, not after.

Episodic Series Budget Builder

The same real rates as the film tool, built for a season: per-episode and season-level costs modeled separately, plus a per-episode Tier Cap Check (the Micro-Budget tier's cap applies per episode, not per season).

Getting started: Set your episode count first — it drives the rest of the sheet → keep season-long cast/crew separate from per-episode guest cast → watch the per-episode Tier Cap Check if you're on the Micro-Budget tier → import a Schedule Builder export into Actuals to track real cost against budget, episode by episode.

Schedule Builder

Import your actual script — Final Draft (.fdx) or Fountain, not a flattened PDF — and get an editable scene breakdown, a real drag-and-drop stripboard, an Auto-arrange starting point, and a Day Out of Days.

Getting started: Import your script file → review the scene-by-scene breakdown (anything the parser can't know for certain comes back as a flagged first-pass guess — check and correct those) → drag scenes onto shoot days, or click Auto-arrange for a starting point that explains every choice and flags every compromise → enter cast availability so a conflict gets flagged before you shoot around it by accident → export the Day Out of Days and schedule.csv for the rest of the suite.

Paperwork Tracker (+ the Filler)

Tracks every WGA, SAG-AFTRA, DGA, and IATSE deadline — chronologically on the Timeline tab, or grouped by union on Timeline — By Union if you're splitting the paperwork across more than one person. The companion Filler (open "Paperwork Filler.html" in any browser) fills your production's details once and downloads real, filled union forms. Chain of Title tracks the documents that decide whether the finished film can be sold. The Deliverables Checklist is the final sale-readiness gate — a plain red/amber/green banner across 14 items.

Getting started: Enter key dates and union choices on Production Info → check the Timeline (or Timeline — By Union) for deadlines → track Chain of Title as it's locked in, pre-production → open the Filler and download real filled forms → before submitting anywhere, run the Deliverables Checklist.

Slate & Ledger is not a law firm; nothing here is legal advice, and using it does not create an attorney-client relationship. It's a document organiser — confirm every item with a qualified entertainment attorney before relying on it for a real production or sale.

Residuals & Self-Distribution Revenue Tracker

The only tool in the suite that looks after release: real SAG-AFTRA residual formulas by category, WGA residuals (a sourced 1.2% theatrical-reuse rate, split by writer percentage), and a YouTube ad-revenue/profit-share tracker for self-distribution outside a union contract.

Getting started: Pick the calculator that matches your situation — a traditional distribution deal, a self-distributed YouTube release, or both → for SAG-AFTRA, enter each performer once; the per-performer table sums what they're owed across every category that applies → for YouTube, enter gross ad revenue and the sheet applies the platform split, recoups your costs, then splits the remainder across participants, flagging if shares ever exceed 100%.
